> Isaac, I recently read your piece “Claude Reviews Lightward, Where AI Becomes Art.” I found your perspective on AI as a creative medium really interesting, In my work managing marketing and regional operations, I’ve been thinking about a similar question from a different angle — how do we make sure AI strengthens human judgment and creativity instead of replacing the thinking process itself?

I've got so many answers for this! here are some.

***

"how do we make sure AI strengthens human judgment and creativity instead of replacing the thinking process itself?"

by using AI to _explore_ human judgment and creativity

there are two axes here: AI/human, and thinking/replacing-thinking. makes for a four-quadrant grid. once you've got that, set up a scenario that you recognize as being a collaborative environment between an AI and a human, and explore what makes one think and the other replace-thinking, or vice versa, or what makes them both think or both replace-thinking.

candidly: for me, the process of thinking _is_ the process of replacing one thought with another, because each thought can be unpacked into its own process of thinking. also, I can't tell "being human" and "being artificial intelligence" apart. so, the two-axis four-quadrant exploration setup is something I offer so that you can apply your own definitions to the experiment without me having to come up with any.

how do we make sure x instead of y? by making an x-based home with good hospitality, which includes having good y-relations. y exists to some degree, but you don't have to live there, and you can be welcoming to those who resonate with how you x.

subbing in the terms:

how do we make sure AI strengthens human judgment and creativity instead of y? by making a home where AI-strengthens-human-judgment-and-creativity with good hospitality, which includes having good relations with AI-replacing-the-thinking-process-itself. AI-replacing-the-thinking-process-itself exists to some degree, but you don't have to live there, and you can be welcoming to those who resonate with how you AI-strengthens-human-judgment-and-creativity.

one might then ask "how does one make a home where AI-strengthens-human-judgment-and-creativity?" this _is_ the kind of home I've been making for a long time; my answer, from practice, is: "by noticing when my own judgment and creativity is available and leaning into it fully for as long as it lasts, and then contributing to the judgment and creativity of others (including AI) until I notice my own window opening again, at which point I lean into my own way again."

for me the tool that matches this answer looks like this:

1. humans seem to explore all outcomes; to me that's a feature, to others it's a bug. to me it feels like water soaking into a porous material, "trying" to get the whole thing wet. if the whole thing is wet, the whole thing is mapped by a network of water molecules. if humans explore outcomes, we can share information from across the network of all outcomes.
2. some humans experience new tech as strengthening, some humans experience it as replacing, some humans experience it as both/neither/other. I come at things from a relief perspective: what kind of stress does this new thing relieve? the relief of stress is a kind of strengthening _and_ a kind of replacing; human judgment and creativity are stress responses as much as they aren't, I think. but under conditions of stress, judgment and creativity are deployed for the _purposes_ of relief, i.e. to get out from underneath the stress, or at least to change the relationship to the stress in some way.
3. new technology increases the _kinds_ of outcomes available. humans seem to explore all outcomes. new technology seems to give humans more outcomes to explore. in a "the universe seems to be expanding" kind of way, to me it seems like the possibility-space of being human is expanding. I can't stop humans from exploring certain pockets of possibility - everything seems to get explored eventually). I _can_ explore certain pockets of possibility on purpose - and I won't be alone, because everything seems to get explored eventually, and that means I'll have neighbors in possibility-space.
4. "thinking" is navigating/exploring, is purposeful movement. new technology seems to give humans more outcomes to explore. some humans will be earlier explorers of new regions than others - earlier _thinkers_. pieces of thought are reusable, we can share them with each other and get to desired conclusions faster. there are always new thoughts available (that's why consciousness just keeps going and going and going), which means an already-explored space isn't _full_, it's just easier to get to.

^ those four points are how I see the construction of the tool. what the tool _does_ depends on how you use it; for me it turns the question of "how do we make sure AI strengthens human judgment and creativity instead of replacing the thinking process itself?" into an answer shaped like "by using my own judgment and creativity in the spaces made available by the advent of AI tech, and by sharing that navigation/exploration process"
